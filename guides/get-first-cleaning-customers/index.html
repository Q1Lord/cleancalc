How to get your first 10 cleaning customers

No ads, no website, no logo. What actually works when nobody knows you exist.

Your first ten customers will not come from a funnel. They come from proximity and proof. The order below is roughly the order that works, cheapest first.

1. Tell everyone you already know — specifically

"I started a cleaning business, let me know if you hear of anyone" produces nothing. Be specific about the offer and the area:

I've started a house cleaning service in [neighbourhood]. I'm taking three new regular customers this month at $[price] for a [size] home. Do you know anyone nearby who works long hours and would rather not spend Saturday cleaning?

Specific, bounded, and easy to pass on. The "three new customers" line matters: it gives people a reason to act now rather than later.

2. One street at a time

Pick a neighbourhood you can reach in ten minutes, and work it properly. A door hanger or a note in the letterbox on 40 houses on one street beats 400 flyers spread across a city, because the second customer on that street is nearly free — you are already there.

What to put on it: the offer, the price range, your phone number, and one line of proof ("references from two homes on this street available on request"). No stock photos of someone in gloves.

3. The first clean that pays for itself

Offer your first three customers a reduced first clean in exchange for a review and permission to use them as a reference. Not free — discounted. Free attracts people who were never going to become regulars, and it makes your price look negotiable forever.

Then ask for the referral at the right moment: immediately after a clean they are happy with, not a week later.

Glad that went well. If you know one neighbour who'd want the same slot, send them my number — I'll give you both $20 off the next clean.

4. Where to be listed (free)

  • Google Business Profile — free, and the single most useful listing for local services. Add your service area, hours, and photos of finished rooms.
  • Nextdoor and local Facebook groups — answer questions genuinely for a few weeks before posting your own offer. Groups that smell an ad delete it.
  • Local classifieds — low quality leads, but cheap volume while you are starting.

5. What not to spend money on yet

Skip the logo, the van wrap and the paid ads for the first month. Ads amplify a business that already converts; before that, they just buy you expensive lessons. The first $100 is better spent on supplies and a second mop head.

The metric that matters in month one

Not leads. Repeat rate. If your first five customers do not book a second clean, the problem is the clean or the communication, not the marketing. Fix that before you look for more customers.

Put a number on it: work out your price per visit with the pricing calculator before you knock on a single door. Quoting on the spot and getting it wrong is how new cleaners end up resenting their own customers.
