Move-out cleaning: checklist and how to price it

The one job where "clean" is defined by someone else — a landlord, an agent, or a deposit.

A move-out clean is not a deep clean of a lived-in home. It is a clean of an empty home, judged against someone else's definition of "professional clean", on a fixed deadline, with money riding on it. That combination makes it the highest-paying and highest-risk job a small cleaner takes.

Why it costs 2–3× a regular clean

  • Everything inside, not just outside. Empty cupboards, inside the oven, inside the fridge, inside wardrobes.
  • Scale and limescale. Vacant properties sit; bathrooms grow deposits that a lived-in home never develops.
  • No furniture to hide behind. Every skirting board, every corner, every window track is visible.
  • It is a deadline job. Keys hand over on a date. There is no "come back next week".
  • Deposit at stake. The customer is paying to protect money, not for comfort — and will inspect hard.

The checklist that satisfies most landlords

AreaWhat is usually expected
KitchenInside oven, hob, extractor, inside fridge/freezer (defrosted), inside all cupboards and drawers, worktops, splashback, sink and taps descaled, floor including edges
Bathroom(s)Shower screen and tiles descaled, grout, bath, toilet including base and behind, sink, mirror, extractor vent, floor edges
Bedrooms / livingInside wardrobes and drawers, skirting boards, door frames, light switches, radiators, inside windows and sills, floors, cobwebs
ThroughoutInside windows (and outside if accessible), window tracks, doors and handles, skirting, light fittings, smoke detector covers
Often forgottenBehind the toilet, top of cupboards, inside the washing machine drawer, balcony/patio, bin store

Ask the customer for the agent's or landlord's own checklist before you start. If one exists, that document overrides yours — and it is the difference between "clean" and "clean enough to release the deposit".

How to price it

Base the price on hours, not on rooms.

  • Empty 2-bed flat, average condition: 6–9 hours
  • Empty 3-bed house: 9–14 hours
  • Heavy condition (smokers, pets, long vacancy): add 30–50%

At $40/hour, a 3-bed house is $360–$560. That sounds high next to a $150 regular clean, and it is — because it is three to four times the work. Do not quote move-outs at recurring rates; you will lose a full day and resent the customer.

Quote it in two stages

Never give a firm number from a description. Offer:

"For an empty 3-bed in average condition, expect $400–$500. I confirm the exact price when I see the property — if it's heavier than described, I'll call you before I start."

Then do a 10-minute viewing, or ask for a video walkthrough. Video is usually enough and saves the trip.

The five mistakes that cost the most

  1. Quoting without seeing it. Vacant properties hide the worst work.
  2. Not asking for the landlord's checklist. You clean to your standard and fail theirs.
  3. No buffer before handover. Book the clean the day before keys go back, not the morning of.
  4. Forgetting the meter cupboard, bin store and balcony. Small areas, easy complaints.
  5. No photo record. Photograph every room when finished — it is your evidence if the deposit is disputed.

Where the work comes from

  • Letting agents — repeat volume, they need reliability more than the lowest price.
  • Property managers — same, with monthly cycles.
  • Airbnb / short-let hosts — turnaround cleans between guests, closer to regular work.
  • Removal companies — they hear "do you know a cleaner?" every week. One conversation, ongoing referrals.

One agent with 20 managed properties is worth more than 20 one-off customers, and they do not haggle over $40 when a deposit release is at stake.
