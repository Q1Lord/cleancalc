What a cleaning quote should include (with a template)

Nine fields that stop the three most common arguments: scope, add-ons and "you said".

A quote is not a price with your logo on it. It is the document that decides whether the customer expects the same job you are planning to do. Most disputes trace back to a quote that was three lines long.

The nine fields

FieldWhy it matters
1. Service address and date"The usual place" is not an address once there are two properties.
2. Scope, room by roomThe single most important part. List what is included and what is not.
3. FrequencyWeekly / fortnightly / monthly / one-time. The price depends on it.
4. Price and what it includesSupplies included or not, travel included or not.
5. Add-ons, priced separatelyInside oven, inside fridge, laundry. Priced, not "we'll see".
6. Estimated durationA range, explicitly marked as an estimate.
7. Access and arrival windowWho lets you in, and the window you will actually keep.
8. Payment termsWhen, how, and what happens if it is late.
9. Validity"Valid for 14 days." Prices change; quotes should not live forever.

A one-page template

Quote #0142 — 12 Oakfield Road
Prepared for: J. Smith · Date: 12 March · Valid until: 26 March

Service
Recurring clean, every two weeks. Approx. 3 hours on site.

Included
Kitchen: counters, sink, stovetop, appliance exteriors, floor.
2 bathrooms: shower, bath, toilet, sink, mirrors, floor.
3 bedrooms and hallway: dusting, vacuuming, mopping, bed making.

Not included
Inside oven, inside fridge, inside cupboards, dishes, laundry, windows above ground floor.

Add-ons (optional)
Inside oven $35 · Inside fridge $30 · Bed linen change $20

Price
$150 per visit, supplies and travel included. First clean quoted separately at $220 due to condition.

Access
Key safe at the side entrance. Arrival window 9:00–9:30.

Payment
Due on the day by card or bank transfer. Late payment: $25 after 7 days.
Cancel with 24 hours' notice at no charge; inside 24 hours, 50% of the visit price.

Quote, estimate, contract — do not mix them up

  • Estimate: a range, before seeing the property. Explicitly provisional.
  • Quote: a fixed price for a defined scope, after a walkthrough or video call.
  • Contract: the agreement covering access, damage, cancellation and termination — the quote is attached to it, not a substitute for it.

Sending a "quote" when you mean an estimate is how you end up honouring a number you invented on the phone.

The line that saves the most arguments

"The price assumes the home is in the condition we discussed. If it is significantly different when I arrive, I will call you before starting, not after — and we agree the new price or reschedule."

Put it in writing on every quote. It converts a potential dispute into a phone call you are happy to make.

How to send it

One page, PDF, sent before the first visit — not dictated on the doorstep. It takes four minutes to prepare once you have a template, and it is the difference between a professional and a person with a mop.

If you are quoting several jobs a week, a spreadsheet and a PDF export is enough. You do not need quoting software until you are sending more than about ten a week.
